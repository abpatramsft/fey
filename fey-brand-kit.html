The mark

A vertex, not a monogram

The logotype is drawn in one continuous chalk weight. The Y is a Feynman vertex — the point where lines meet, where particles are created. One amber dot marks it. That dot is the only spot of colour the wordmark needs.

The grammar

Two lines carry the whole system

Every Feynman diagram is built from two kinds of line. fey borrows them as its two verbs — so the visual language and the product map to each other exactly.

Fermion — a directed line

fey:create

Solid, with an arrow: it goes somewhere. You make the thing — the wiki, the diagrams, the commit rationale — and understanding follows creation.

Photon — an exchange

fey:optimise

Wavy, no arrow: it's a back-and-forth. fey trades against the metrics it finds and hill-climbs — tightening code and commit history without losing the thread.

Palette · colored chalk on slate

Drawn, not printed

A blackboard green — warmer and softer than pure black — with chalk white for lines and text. Two pieces of colored chalk do all the accent work, one per verb.

Commands

Two verbs, one philosophy

Everything fey does resolves to one of two commands. Amber creates; sky refines. Keep them in mono, always lowercase, always with the colon.

fey:create fey:optimise

create builds the wiki, draws the API flows as Feynman diagrams, and reconstructs commit rationale. optimise reads the metrics and hill-climbs on the code and the history.
